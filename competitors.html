Competitive landscape

Where Fit Check fits in a crowded field

This is a competitive-landscape page I built from public research while making this prototype for an internship application. It is not an official HexCoded comparison and carries no endorsement from any company named on it. Every competitor here is a serious product built for a real job; the point of the page is to show where a pre-render conversion-intelligence layer would sit among them, not to argue that anyone else is doing their job badly.

The other direction

Where this research found HexCoded behind

The table above isn't meant to read as a clean sweep — here's what the same research turned up in the other direction.

Behind
Language coverage
HexCoded states 70+ languages. HeyGen states 175+, with dubbing across all of them. On this specific axis HeyGen is simply ahead — more than double the coverage — and for a brand whose priority is reaching many markets in-language, that difference is material enough to decide the choice on its own.
Unconfirmed
Free-tier availability
HeyGen, Creatify and Synthesia all publicly offer some form of free tier or trial, so a team can evaluate them before paying. HexCoded's own site describes "simple, honest pricing" but does not publicly confirm whether a free tier exists. That is a gap in the information available to this research, not a confirmed deficiency — it may well exist and simply not be stated where this research looked.

Neither point changes where Fit Check's own gap analysis lands — no competitor researched has a pre-render fit-scoring layer — but a comparison that only points one way isn't a comparison, it's an advertisement.

Read fairly

What each one is actually built for

Most of these differences are positioning, not quality. A tool aimed at corporate training is not "worse" than one aimed at paid social — it is aimed elsewhere.

Arcads
A large AI-actor library reading hook-style scripts to camera, built specifically for high-volume performance-marketing ad testing. Premium pricing, no free tier — priced like a performance tool rather than a trial product.
HeyGen
Strong avatar realism and 175+ language dubbing. Built more for spokesperson, explainer and corporate video than for scroll-native ad creative. A free tier makes it the easiest of these to try.
Creatify
A URL-to-ad pipeline: point it at a product page and get ad variations back quickly, with a built-in editor. Focused on ecommerce volume — closest in shape to HexCoded's own URL → Ad tool.
Synthesia
Enterprise and corporate video with a broad avatar library, oriented around training and internal communication rather than ad creative.
Multi-model studios
A small number of newer platforms generating across several underlying models from one subscription — the closest structural peers to HexCoded's sixteen-models-one-subscription approach.
The white space

What Fit Check specifically adds

HexCoded already competes without Fit Check. It aggregates sixteen frontier models — video and image — under one subscription rather than making a brand pick and pay for a single vendor. It offers three actor tiers, including consent-based licensing for real actors, where most competitors researched offer synthetic AI actors only. And it already treats technical-failure waste as solved: failed renders are free, a policy most competitors do not state at all.

What none of them appeared to have is a pre-render, outcome-learning score for whether a particular actor will convert for a particular product. Across Arcads, HeyGen, Creatify, Synthesia and the multi-model studios, choosing an actor means browsing a library or sorting by popularity. Popularity is a usage signal: the actors who get picked most are the ones who get picked most. It says nothing about whether that actor actually converted for skincare, or for consumer electronics.

That is the gap Fit Check aims at, and it is the same problem the main site opens with. A render that fails technically already costs nothing. The expensive case is the render that succeeds technically and still underperforms, because the pairing was wrong — and that one bills in full. A score computed before the credits are spent, which sharpens as real outcomes are logged against it, is a different kind of signal from a library sorted by popularity. As of this research, nobody in this set was surfacing one.

Honest accounting

How to read this page

Caveat
Public information, gathered once
Everything here comes from publicly available material collected while building this prototype. Nothing was obtained from any company directly, and no competitor reviewed or approved a word of it.
Caveat
This moves quickly
Pricing tiers, avatar libraries and feature sets in this space change on a scale of weeks. Anything stated here should be re-checked before it is relied on. Pricing is deliberately described qualitatively rather than with figures that would go stale.
Caveat
Scoped to what was researched
The claim is that this research did not find a pre-render fit-scoring layer in this set of products — not that it exists nowhere, nor that nobody will build one. It is a snapshot, and a narrow one.
Intent
Market awareness, not disparagement
The purpose is to show that this prototype was built with an understanding of the field it would enter. Each product named here is well built for the job it targets. Being aimed at a different job is a positioning difference, not a flaw.
